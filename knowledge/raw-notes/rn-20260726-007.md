# Integration Behavior Tests Domain-Boundary Hypotheses

## Note

A domain boundary remains a hypothesis until its runtime interactions have been
examined.

The practitioner uses provisional integration design to expose responsibilities
or dependencies that the domain model alone may hide. Relevant observations
include synchronous call chains, fan-out, repeated lookup, partial updates,
reconciliation, duplicated transformation, failure propagation, and coordinated
ownership.

When the interaction appears unnatural, the response is not limited to adding
integration mechanisms. The practitioner revisits the original boundary and
responsibility placement.

This runtime simulation is distinct from business-change simulation. The former
examines requests, events, failures, consistency, and recovery. The latter
examines how future business decisions propagate through code, contracts,
knowledge, and teams.

A useful boundary should help localize both forms of change.

Boundary design and Integration design are therefore one iterative capability,
not two independent sequential activities:

```text
form a Boundary hypothesis
  -> design the interaction
  -> inspect communication, Data, Failure, and completion dependencies
  -> revise the Integration or the Boundary
  -> validate again
```

An architect who can propose Domain Boundaries but cannot design the
connections may distort the Boundaries to avoid Integration problems.
Conversely, Integration mechanisms can conceal an invalid Boundary rather than
correct it.
