# Business-Change Scenarios Test Boundary Locality

## Note

After proposing bounded contexts, the practitioner mentally applies plausible
future business changes to them.

Examples include changes to products, pricing, customer segments, channels,
campaigns, approval rules, regulations, and exception policies.

The simulation observes where the change lands, who owns the business outcome,
which teams need the relevant knowledge, whether rules must be duplicated, and
whether coordinated releases become necessary.

A change affecting several services is not automatically evidence of a bad
boundary. It may be acceptable when contexts respond independently through
stable contracts and do not duplicate the same business decision.

The warning condition is a single business decision that requires multiple
services or teams to possess the same knowledge and change together.

## Questions

- Which forms of cross-context change are legitimate collaboration?
- How should likely future changes be selected without overfitting speculation?
