# Strategic Plans and Abandoned Needs Ground Change Scenarios

## Note

This note records an interview response to a question in `rn-20260726-006` and
qualifies `rn-20260726-020`.

The practitioner's ability to evaluate User Story value slicing depends partly
on prior Product Management experience. It should not be treated as a
mechanical technique that every architecture practitioner can apply with the
same reliability.

For boundary stress-testing, the practitioner selects future Business Change
scenarios from a mid-term management plan and predicts additional changes that
are plausible within that strategic context.

This future-oriented interpretation is also a practitioner strength. When
customers have difficulty inventing concrete future scenarios, the practitioner
uses a more accessible evidence source:

> Identify a need that previously existed but was abandoned because of system
> constraints, then test the proposed boundaries against that need.

Previously abandoned needs are concrete enough for stakeholders to recall and
discuss. They expose change pressure that the current system could not
accommodate without depending entirely on speculative future requirements.

The two scenario sources represent different evidence:

- strategic plans and their context indicate expected future demand; and
- abandoned needs indicate demonstrated but unrealized demand.

## Questions

- How should scenarios inferred from strategic context be validated?
- When has an abandoned need become too obsolete to remain useful?
- How should scenario selection work when neither strategic plans nor remembered
  abandoned needs are available?
