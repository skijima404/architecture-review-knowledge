# Boundary Hypotheses Consider Business Handoffs and Developer Knowledge Load

## Note

The practitioner considers at least three axes together when forming bounded
context hypotheses:

1. Value Stream cohesion
2. Domain Knowledge cohesion
3. The distribution of work and knowledge across people and teams

The third axis is examined from two different perspectives.

### Domain-Expert Responsibility and Handover

The first perspective concerns how domain experts divide business-design and
operational responsibilities.

The practitioner considers how the work would be divided if they were designing
the business responsibilities themselves. This includes identifying where one
person or group could hand work over to another without requiring both sides to
retain the same unresolved business judgment.

The objective is not simply to create more organizational divisions. The
practitioner looks for the smallest meaningful points at which responsibility,
knowledge, and the expected outcome can be handed over.

These handover points may provide evidence for business and domain boundaries,
but they do not mechanically determine bounded contexts.

### Developer-Team Knowledge Load

The second perspective concerns how much Domain Knowledge a development team
must receive and retain in order to make correct changes.

The practitioner considers:

- which Domain Knowledge should remain within one development team;
- where a team is likely to become overloaded while absorbing additional
  Domain Knowledge;
- whether the same Domain Knowledge must be transferred to two or more teams;
- whether multiple teams must coordinate to interpret or change the same
  business decision; and
- whether a team can understand, change, and operate its responsibility without
  depending on knowledge held elsewhere.

This reasoning does not primarily optimize developer utilization or attempt to
fill each team's available capacity.

A boundary may be valuable even when it does not produce perfectly utilized
teams. The primary concern is whether Domain Knowledge can be transferred,
retained, and applied without cognitive overload or unnecessary duplication
across development teams.

Technical similarity is therefore not sufficient reason to combine
responsibilities. Likewise, organizational capacity alone is not sufficient
reason to split them.

## Questions

- What makes a handover point meaningful and sustainable for domain experts?
- How can impending Domain Knowledge overload in a development team be
  observed?
- When is sharing Domain Knowledge across multiple development teams legitimate?
- How should boundaries respond when business handovers and developer knowledge
  boundaries do not align?
