# Architecture Vision Supplies Boundary Evaluation Criteria

## Note

This note records interview responses to questions in `rn-20260726-001`,
`rn-20260726-002`, and `rn-20260726-003`.

An Architecture Vision is required before meaningful microservice boundary
design. The Vision may be provisional or explicitly hypothetical; it does not
need to be a formally approved document.

The Vision must explain which problem Microservices Architecture is intended to
address and what value the architecture is expected to create. Without that
value hypothesis, a plausible decomposition can still be produced, but there
are no adequate criteria for deciding how the system should be divided or
whether the result is useful.

At least one of the following evidence perspectives is also required:

- system-side knowledge, including project knowledge, application structure,
  and code; or
- business-side knowledge, including practitioner knowledge and business
  manuals.

This evidence should support a Big Picture, Process Modeling, or both. When only
one perspective is available, modeling can proceed, but uncertainty remains in
the missing perspective.

Mid-term business plans, Business Roadmaps, and IT Roadmaps improve boundary
design by exposing expected change. Operational capabilities and current
technology processes improve the realism of technology choices.

## Questions

- What minimum form must a provisional Architecture Vision take?
- How should uncertainty from a missing business or system perspective be made
  visible in a boundary proposal?
