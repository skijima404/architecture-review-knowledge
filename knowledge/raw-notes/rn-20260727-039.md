# Business Deadlines Define Timing, Ordering, and Freshness Requirements

## Note

The practitioner evaluates East-West timing from Business Needs rather than
assuming that lower Latency is always better.

### Timing

The analysis distinguishes:

- Hard Real-time;
- Near Real-time;
- tolerable communication Latency; and
- the final Business Deadline.

Duplicate delivery, Retry, replay, and other asynchronous behavior are examined
when they can make a Real-time Use Case fail.

If a strict requirement applies to all communication between two Services, the
practitioner questions whether a Network Boundary is sustainable. If it applies
only to a specific Use Case, the practitioner first investigates whether the
separation can be preserved for the other Use Cases. Concrete solutions remain
case-specific. When no economically rational design can maintain the
separation, the Boundary is integrated.

### Ordering

The visual order of Events in Event Storming is not sufficient evidence of a
Business Ordering invariant. Domain Experts are asked concrete reversal
questions:

> If B completes before A and A arrives several minutes later, what Business
> harm occurs?

For User-initiated processing, the relevant order may be when the Request
entered the overall System, not when it reached an individual Microservice.

The design may therefore distinguish:

```text
accepted_at
  -> original System-boundary acceptance time

event_created_at
  -> time an individual Event was produced

processed_at
  -> time a Consumer processed it
```

When `accepted_at` defines Business order, it is established once at ingress
and propagated unchanged. Concrete partitioning, clock, tie-breaking, and
reordering mechanisms remain implementation decisions.

### Freshness

When immediate Data is not required, the practitioner asks:

- when the Business activity occurs;
- how frequently it occurs;
- which decision consumes the Data; and
- what later activity or cutoff requires the Data to be available.

This is especially relevant for analytical Data. A five-minute freshness target
is not useful when nobody performs a corresponding near-real-time decision.

```text
Business activity cadence
  -> decision timing
  -> required Data freshness
  -> Integration latency target
```

### Batch Is Not the Automatic Alternative

The absence of a Real-time requirement does not make Batch the default.

Batch can concentrate:

- network and database load into a night-time window;
- completion pressure into a fixed timebox;
- failure recovery into the same constrained window; and
- delay across downstream Batch dependencies.

The practitioner adopts Batch after confirming sufficient bandwidth, Capacity,
completion time, and recovery headroom. Continuous Event or Queue processing
may instead smooth load while still meeting the Business Deadline.

## Questions

- Which Business deadlines are implicit in downstream activities?
- Which ordering constraints apply to a specific Entity rather than globally?
- Which Batch windows have enough failure-recovery headroom?
