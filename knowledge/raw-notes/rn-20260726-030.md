# Business-Architecture-First Reasoning Depends on Practitioner Background

## Note

This note records the applicability boundary identified at the end of the
interview that produced `rn-20260726-009` through `rn-20260726-029`.

The practitioner's Architecture Reasoning often begins from Business
Architecture. This is supported by an atypical combination of experience and
knowledge for a software architect:

- Product Management experience;
- familiarity with company processes;
- familiarity with responsibility division across organizational units; and
- experience reasoning about business design and governance.

This background supports intuitive detection of mismatches among stakeholder
needs, business responsibility, Product Ownership, Development Teams, and
system boundaries.

The resulting methods must not be presented as equally accessible to every
architect. Other practitioners may need different evidence sources,
collaborators, structured prompts, or explicit Business Architecture support to
reach comparable conclusions.

Practitioner intuition remains useful for generating investigation hypotheses.
It is not independently reproducible evidence and must be checked through
artifacts, interviews, Domain Experts, or other observations.

The interview was intentionally stopped before converting every open question
into a generalized rule. Unanswered questions remain valid Raw Note uncertainty.
