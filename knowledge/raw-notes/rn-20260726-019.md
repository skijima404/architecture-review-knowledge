# Responsibility-Scope Mismatch Suggests Boundary Misalignment

## Note

This note records an interview response to a question in `rn-20260726-005` and
continues `rn-20260726-018`.

After technical investigation produces a hypothesis about code complexity, the
practitioner uses stakeholder interviews to test whether the complexity reflects
Engineering Debt or a mismatch in Bounded Context and responsibility placement.

The initial recognition of a possible mismatch relies substantially on
practitioner intuition and prior pattern recognition.

One recurring pattern is orchestration logic placed in a project-management
system. The orchestrator may encode detailed knowledge belonging to downstream
Domains even though the people responsible for project management care mainly
that the downstream result is achieved, not how the downstream process makes
its decisions.

The problem is not orchestration as an interaction style by itself. The concern
is that a Context implements knowledge and decisions outside the responsibility
and scope of the Domain it represents.

The practitioner investigates:

- the responsibility of the people and Domain represented by the Context;
- the intended scope of that responsibility;
- whether the implementation feels inconsistent with that scope;
- which external Domain Knowledge has been embedded there; and
- whether another Domain already owns, or should own, the displaced
  responsibility.

Interviews are used to validate the responsibility and scope assumptions rather
than treating code structure alone as proof of boundary misalignment.

## Questions

- How can legitimate cross-domain orchestration be distinguished from displaced
  Domain Knowledge?
- What interview evidence is sufficient to confirm responsibility ownership?
- Which signs of scope mismatch can be made reproducible beyond practitioner
  intuition?
