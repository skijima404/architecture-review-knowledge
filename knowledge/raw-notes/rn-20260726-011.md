# Concern Origin Separates Code Complexity from Domain Importance

## Note

This note records interview responses to questions in `rn-20260726-002` and
`rn-20260726-005`.

When assessment output and Domain Expert input appear to conflict, the
practitioner first checks whether both outputs are valid and whether they
describe the same concern.

Technical assessment often reveals Technology Architecture concerns. Domain
Experts express Business Architecture needs, but their requested solution must
also be checked against the underlying business outcome they intend to achieve.

Code complexity and business importance frequently do not match. Complexity may
instead originate from accumulated modifications, numerous data-transformation
patterns, or an upstream identity and access design. Code complexity reveals a
concern but does not by itself identify the concern's origin or strategic
importance.

Assessment allows the practitioner to form a hypothesis about which parts
developers perceive as the Core Domain. A later DDD workshop with Domain Experts
can test whether those areas contain essential business complexity or whether
Technology Architecture concerns have leaked into application code.

Once the concern's origin is understood, the architecture can decide whether it
belongs in the application design, an integration responsibility, a platform
capability, or another corrective mechanism.

## Questions

- What evidence is sufficient to assign a concern to a platform rather than an
  application?
- How should a genuine conflict between Business Architecture and Technology
  Architecture constraints be represented?
