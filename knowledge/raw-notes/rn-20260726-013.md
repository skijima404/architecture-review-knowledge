# Operational Participation and Data Enable Architecture Transformation

## Note

This note records an interview response to a question in `rn-20260726-003`.

The minimum Operational Capability is not a particular tool. Operations must
have actual human capacity, and at least one Operations participant must be
willing to engage in the transformation or act as a continuing counterpart for
the architecture work.

Even a strong architecture is difficult to sustain when nobody on the
Operations side can reason about and participate in the corresponding
operating-model transformation.

This practitioner's architecture process also emphasizes hypothesis
validation. Observability is interpreted broadly to include monitoring,
incident data, configuration-management data, and other operational state and
change information. Operational conditions should be represented as data where
practical so that architecture and operating-model hypotheses can be evaluated
and corrected.

Operational participation enables the transformation. Operational data enables
continuing hypothesis validation.

## Questions

- What minimum participation commitment is required from Operations?
- Which operational data must exist before implementation, and which can be
  created during transition?
