# Context Maps Expose Design-Time Responsibility Concentration

## Note

This note records an interview response to a question in `rn-20260726-008`.
Public source `src-20260726-002` describes the associated workshop design.

During Microservices decomposition, production telemetry does not yet exist.
The work is normally forming Target Information Systems Architecture or
preparing its handoff into Technology Architecture.

At this stage, the practitioner layers Domain, Application, and Data boundaries
and their major dependencies onto a Context Map. The map is used to detect
concentrated or unexplained responsibility before implementation.

Investigation signals include:

- dependencies concentrated in one Microservice without an explainable reason;
- circular or bidirectional dependencies;
- changes that require simultaneous modification of multiple Microservices;
- a Shared Kernel without an unavoidable shared-model responsibility;
- unusually many or few use cases, consumers, actors, interfaces, or data
  attributes;
- information with different lifecycles collected in one responsibility; and
- a Microservice whose responsibility or reason for existence cannot be stated
  clearly.

The presence of a bias does not prove that a boundary is wrong. A Business or
Technology reason may justify it. When the reason cannot be explained, the
Boundary, Responsibility, Data Ownership, or Integration Model is revisited.

Unresolved uncertainty is retained for Domain Expert review, PoC, implementation
validation, or production Observability rather than being hidden by declaring
the boundary final.

## Questions

- Which Context Map biases most reliably predict implementation problems?
- How should justified concentration be documented for later verification?
- Which uncertainties must be resolved before Technology Architecture begins?
