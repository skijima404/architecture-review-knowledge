# Product-Responsibility Paths Are Compared with Domain and System Boundaries

## Note

This note records an interview response to a question in `rn-20260726-027`.
Source `src-20260726-003` identifies a user-provided book excerpt that prompted
the explanation.

The practitioner first examines whether product responsibilities are divided
coherently across the organization. In a large system, Product Owners or Product
Managers are expected to divide responsibility at some meaningful product or
problem-domain level.

When the organization is logically aligned, a requirement can follow a
relatively direct responsibility path:

```text
Domain Expert
  -> Product Owner
  -> Development Team
  -> Responsible System Area
```

This is a logical traceability path, not an assertion that reporting lines and
software boundaries must be identical or that real organizations always achieve
this structure.

When the path is coherent, the practitioner examines whether knowledge and
intent are transferred correctly between the Domain Expert and Product Owner.

When the organization is not coherently aligned, the practitioner uses Event
Storming to identify where each requirement originates. The resulting
requirement is compared with:

- the responsible Domain Expert;
- the relevant Subdomain or business boundary;
- Product Ownership;
- the Development Team; and
- the affected system responsibility.

A mismatch between those elements is a reason to investigate responsibility,
knowledge transfer, and boundary alignment.

This analysis depends substantially on the practitioner's ability to reason from
Business Architecture. It may be difficult to reproduce from software structure
or team structure alone.

## Questions

- Which Domain Expert-to-Product Owner interaction patterns reveal broken intent
  transfer?
- How should product responsibility be assessed when one Product Owner spans
  several Subdomains?
- Which mismatches can be tolerated without changing the organization or system
  boundary?
