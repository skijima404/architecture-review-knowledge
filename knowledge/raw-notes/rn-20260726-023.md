# Valid Boundaries Precede Integration-Pattern Optimization

## Note

This note records an interview response to a question in `rn-20260726-007` and
continues `rn-20260726-022`.

Before absorbing complexity with an Integration pattern, the practitioner first
confirms that the business boundary is appropriate and that there is a valid
reason to keep the responsibilities separated.

The practitioner then distinguishes the communication direction.

- For North-South communication, such as frontend-to-backend interaction, the
  practitioner's default is an API.
- For East-West communication between services, the practitioner's default is
  asynchronous interaction.

These are personal design defaults, not universal rules.

Integration-side mitigation is appropriate when the business boundary remains
valid and separation protects a real concern. Examples include independent
capacity requirements and multiple components repeatedly accessing the same
table or attribute even though the data model is appropriate for the business.

The practitioner compares widely shared data with identity-management design,
where authoritative information is commonly distributed for use by multiple
consumers. Data with this kind of broadly shared role may need to be handled
through Integration mechanisms rather than forcing all consumers back into one
business boundary.

The reasoning order is:

1. validate the business boundary;
2. validate the reason for separation;
3. identify North-South or East-West interaction;
4. identify data ownership, sharing, capacity, and consistency needs; and
5. select an Integration pattern.

## Questions

- What exceptions override the API and asynchronous defaults?
- What evidence is sufficient to confirm that a business boundary is correct?
- Which consistency requirements prevent distribution or local copies of shared
  data?

## Next Steps

Integration pattern used to distribute it.

## Corrections

- 2026-07-26: Follow-up research and practitioner clarification established
  that North-South and East-West are standard traffic classifications, and that
  asynchronous East-West integration is a widely recommended Microservices
  default rather than only a personal preference. The practitioner's method is
  to investigate why an East-West interaction cannot be asynchronous, not to
  require asynchronous communication without examining business constraints.
  See `rn-20260726-024` and `src-20260726-001`.
