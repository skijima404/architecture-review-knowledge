# Modeling Evidence Selection Depends on Practitioner Strengths

## Note

This note records an interview response to questions in `rn-20260726-001`.

The simplified modeling techniques used in a DDD workshop remain useful in
practice. Practitioner methods are additional options rather than replacements
for those techniques.

Modeling depends partly on the practitioner's cognitive tendencies, especially
which information they recognize and use as decision evidence. This practitioner
is particularly comfortable reasoning from Business Architecture and the
intended business design.

Business manuals and other operational documents can be used as primary inputs
for discovering Domain Knowledge. The resulting business interpretation is
combined with technical assessment evidence such as code condition, change
history, and application characteristics.

This approach may be less effective for a practitioner who is not comfortable
reasoning from Business Architecture. The generalizable part may be the use of
business-operational material as domain-modeling evidence; the practitioner's
ability to interpret that evidence is not automatically transferable.

## Questions

- Which alternative evidence-selection approaches work for practitioners with
  different cognitive strengths?
- What checks prevent a practitioner's preferred evidence source from biasing
  the resulting model?
