# Domain-Expert Handover Uses Input-Judgment-Output Completeness

## Note

This note records an interview response to a question in `rn-20260726-004`.

When finer business-responsibility decomposition is needed, the practitioner
looks for a locally complete combination of input, judgment or operation, and
output. Such a sequence is a candidate unit that one person or group could hand
over to another.

Formal separability is not sufficient. The practitioner also considers the
handover overhead and the relative independence of each unit.

In a generalized invoice example, writing a line-item name and recording its
quantity are tightly related and expensive to separate. Calculating a subtotal
from a unit price and quantity is more separable. Calculating the final total is
more independent than either of those earlier activities.

The practitioner first investigates how finely the work could be divided, then
decides whether that division is useful. This high-resolution analysis is
mentally demanding and is used when finer decomposition than the current model
is actually needed. A separable unit is not automatically a desirable
organizational boundary.
