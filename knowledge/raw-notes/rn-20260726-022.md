# Integration Complexity Is Diagnosed by Sequence and Dependency Shape

## Note

This note records an interview response to a question in `rn-20260726-007` and
relates to the business-cohesion reasoning in `rn-20260726-014`.

When Integration appears complex, the practitioner first checks business
cohesion. A candidate business unit has a locally coherent input, judgment or
operation, and output. Splitting tightly related activities creates handover
overhead, while more independent outcomes are easier to separate.

The practitioner then asks whether the model is wrong or the technical
implementation has introduced the complexity.

Business processes are often already divided into workable responsibilities.
Poor business separation may appear operationally as two people or groups
needing to work continuously beside one another. The practitioner still checks
business cohesion, but initially considers the technical side more likely to
contain the defect.

Technical Integration Complexity is examined in at least two shapes.

## Sequence Complexity

Repeated back-and-forth interaction between the same Microservices in one
sequence suggests that the system may have been divided too finely or along the
wrong boundary.

The practitioner returns to Event Storming and investigates why the
responsibilities were separated. Business cohesion is used as the comparison
surface for evaluating the technical boundary.

## Diagram Complexity

A service with many dependencies on a diagram may indicate:

- reference or master data that is legitimately needed in many places; or
- insufficient responsibility decomposition.

For frequently referenced data, the practitioner considers synchronizing the
minimum necessary local copy into the application that needs it.

When insufficient decomposition is suspected, Event Storming is used to inspect
which table attributes are required by which business activities and decisions.
The purpose is to identify the actual knowledge and responsibility dependency,
not to infer a boundary directly from tables.

These are investigation heuristics rather than automatic conclusions.

### Observable Boundary Instability

The practitioner considers the following easier to observe than the underlying
Business Architecture distortion:

- the same Data repeatedly crossing the same Boundary;
- two Services changing together;
- two Services requiring the same Release; and
- a locally dense cluster of Runtime communication.

These signals do not prove that the Boundary is wrong. The practitioner traces
them back to Context, Responsibility, and Knowledge ownership.

Version growth can provide a related but less direct signal. When External
Contract Versions grow while Runtime communication remains natural, the
practitioner suspects an API as a Product and Developer Experience problem
rather than a Microservice Boundary problem. See `rn-20260727-038`.

## Questions

- When is repeated service interaction inherent to the business sequence?
- What consistency and freshness conditions constrain local copies of reference
  data?
- How can diagram complexity caused by shared data be distinguished from a
  distributed business decision?
